POSTULATES

13 ITEMS
73

POSTULATE OF FUNDAMENTAL CATEGORIES

Definition: The universe of ideas can be analysed through fundamental categories used for facet analysis. Ranganathan recognizes five: Personality (P) Matter (M) Energy (E) Space (S) Time (T)
Example: A subject can be analysed into combinations of these fundamental categories.
74

POSTULATE OF BASIC FACET

Definition: A facet that represents the basic structure of a subject should be identified as the basic facet for that subject.
Example: In analysing a subject, the principal facet that establishes the subject's basic identity is distinguished from the other facets.
75

POSTULATE OF ISOLATE FACET

Definition: An isolate facet is a facet containing isolate ideas that qualify or further specify the subject.
Example: In a subject such as "treatment of cancer patients in India in 2025", place, time and treatment-related concepts may function as isolate facets.
76

POSTULATE OF ROUNDS FOR ENERGY

Definition: When Energy facets recur in different rounds of a compound subject, their occurrence and sequence are governed through the concept of rounds.
Example: A subject may involve one energy/action followed by another action at a later level of the subject structure.
77

POSTULATE OF ROUNDS FOR PERSONALITY AND MATTER

Definition: Personality and Matter facets may occur in successive rounds when the structure of a compound subject requires their recurrence.
Example: A subject may involve more than one Personality or Matter component at different structural levels.
78

POSTULATE OF ROUND FOR SPACE AND TIME

Definition: Space and Time facets may be organized in rounds where they recur in the structure of the subject.
Example: A subject may refer to one geographical context and one time context and then contain a further spatial/temporal qualification.
79

POSTULATE OF LEVEL

Definition: Facets and their components may occur at different levels in the structure of a compound subject; these levels must be recognized when determining facet sequence.
Example: A broad subject may contain progressively deeper levels of specification.
80

POSTULATE FOR FACET SEQUENCE

Definition: The facets of a subject should be arranged according to a determinate and principled sequence rather than an arbitrary order.
Example: Once the appropriate facet sequence is determined for a class of subjects, the same basis is applied consistently.
81

POSTULATE OF FIRST FACET

Definition: The first facet in the sequence should be determined according to the prescribed rules of facet sequence rather than arbitrary choice.
Example: The facet selected as first is chosen because of its structural/conceptual priority.
82

POSTULATE OF CONCRETENESS

Definition: In determining facet sequence, the more concrete facet should receive appropriate priority according to the rules governing concreteness.
Example: A facet representing a concrete entity may precede a more abstract qualification where the prescribed sequence requires it.
83

POSTULATE OF FACET SEQUENCE WITHIN A ROUND

Definition: When several facets occur within the same round, their sequence should be determined according to the prescribed principles for ordering facets within that round.
Example: Several facets belonging to one round are arranged in a fixed principled order rather than randomly.
84

POSTULATE OF FACET SEQUENCE WITHIN LAST ROUND

Definition: The facets occurring in the last round should have a definite sequence appropriate to their role in completing the compound subject.
Example: Final qualifying facets are arranged in the prescribed order so that the completed subject receives a consistent notation.
85

POSTULATE OF LEVEL CLUSTER

Definition: Facets or components belonging to the same level may be treated as a cluster for determining their appropriate sequence.
Example: Related facets occurring at one level of a compound subject are kept together rather than being scattered arbitrarily.

PRINCIPLES FOR FACET SEQUENCE

4 ITEMS
86

WALL-PICTURE PRINCIPLE

Definition: The order of facets should correspond to the natural way in which the components of a subject are conceptually seen together, analogous to the arrangement of a picture on a wall.
Example: In arranging related facets, the sequence should present the subject in a natural coherent view rather than an arbitrary order.
87

WHOLE-ORGAN PRINCIPLE

Definition: When facets represent parts or organs of a whole, their sequence should reflect the relationship of the parts to the whole and follow an appropriate natural/functional order.
Example: When classifying organs of an organism, their sequence can follow the organization of the whole organism rather than an arbitrary order.
88

COW-CALF PRINCIPLE

Definition: When two facets have a close natural dependency, the dependent facet should follow the facet on which it naturally depends, analogous to the relationship of a cow and its calf.
Example: A dependent component should not be separated arbitrarily from the component with which it has the closest natural relationship.
89

ACTAND–ACTION–ACTOR–TOOL PRINCIPLE

Definition: Where a subject involves an action, its facets may be sequenced according to the conceptual relationship among: Actand → Action → Actor → Tool
Example: In a subject concerning treatment, the thing acted upon, the treatment/action, the person performing it and the instrument used can be related in this conceptual sequence.
