1.1 LAWS 6
1 LAW OF INTERPRETATION
Example
EXAMPLE
When a term occurs in a classification
schedule, its meaning is determined in the
context of the scheme and its relationship
with neighbouring classes, rather than
simply by ordinary dictionary meaning.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
2 LAW OF IMPARTIALITY
Example
EXAMPLE
The sequence of facets in different subjects
should not be decided arbitrarily in each
subject. A general principle should be
applied impartially to comparable cases.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
3 LAW OF SYMMETRY
Example
EXAMPLE
If two corresponding classes or devices have
equivalent roles in a classification system,
they should receive corresponding treatment
rather than giving one arbitrary preference.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
4 LAW OF PARSIMONY
Example
EXAMPLE
If two classification arrangements accomplish
the same purpose, the one requiring less
memory, less mental effort or less complicated
operations is preferred.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
5 LAW OF LOCAL VARIATION
Example
EXAMPLE
A classification principle normally applied
generally may be modified in a particular
subject or local situation when the special
requirements of that situation make the
variation desirable.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
6 LAW OF OSMOSIS
Example
EXAMPLE
A useful classificatory device developed for
one area may gradually be adopted in other
areas when it proves suitable there.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘

should facilitate the actual use of books,
rather than merely preserving them as objects
that are difficult for readers to reach.

subject and level of study.

A specialized scientific book may have only
a small audience, but classification and
arrangement should enable that audience to
discover and use it.

unnecessary searching.

3.1.1 CANONS FOR CHARACTERISTICS 4
12 CANON OF DIFFERENTIATION
Example
EXAMPLE
If books are to be divided into Indian and
British literature, "country/origin" is a
differentiating characteristic because it
separates the two groups.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
13 CANON OF RELEVANCE
Example
EXAMPLE
In classifying books by subject, subject
characteristics are relevant; an unrelated
characteristic such as the colour of the
book cover would normally be irrelevant.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
14 CANON OF ASCERTAINABILITY
Example
EXAMPLE
If place of publication is used as a
characteristic, it should be possible to
determine the place from reliable information
about the document.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
15 CANON OF PERMANENCE
Example
EXAMPLE
A stable subject characteristic is preferable
to a temporary feature that may soon cease to
be useful for arranging the literature.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
3.1.2 CANONS FOR SUCCESSION OF CHARACTERISTICS 3
16 CANON OF CONCOMITANCE
Example
EXAMPLE
If the presence of one characteristic
necessarily accompanies another in the
universe being classified, their treatment
should recognize that relationship rather
than creating an artificial independent
succession.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
17 CANON OF RELEVANT SUCCESSION
Example
EXAMPLE
When several characteristics can successively
divide a universe, their order should be
selected according to their usefulness for
the intended classification.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
18 CANON OF CONSISTENT SUCCESSION
Example
EXAMPLE
If a scheme establishes Subject → Place →
Time as its succession, the same succession
should normally be maintained wherever the
same conditions apply.
3.1.3 CANONS FOR ARRAY 4
19 CANON OF EXHAUSTIVENESS
Example
BOOK EXAMPLE
BCI lists special kinds of libraries and then
provides a residual class such as "other
special kinds of libraries." This ensures
that the array covers the universe.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
20 CANON OF EXCLUSIVENESS
Example
EXAMPLE
If an array divides books by language into
English, French and German, the divisions
should not overlap on that same characteristic
basis.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
21 CANON OF HELPFUL SEQUENCE
Example
EXAMPLE
Historical subjects may be arranged from
earlier to later periods when chronological
order is most useful to users.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
22 CANON OF CONSISTENT SEQUENCE
Example
EXAMPLE
If a set of subjects is arranged in the order
Physics → Chemistry → Biology in one relevant
array, the corresponding repeated group
should follow the same order elsewhere.

3.2.1 VERBAL CANONS 4
27 CANON OF CONTEXT
Example
EXAMPLE
A word such as "culture" may have different
meanings in ordinary language. Its meaning
in a classification schedule must be fixed
by the context of the classes around it.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
28 CANON OF ENUMERATION
Example
EXAMPLE
A schedule should specify what a class term
includes when ordinary language would leave
its scope uncertain.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
29 CANON OF CURRENCY
Example
EXAMPLE
When a discipline replaces an obsolete term
with an accepted modern technical term, the
classification terminology should recognize
the current usage.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
30 CANON OF RETICENCE
Example
EXAMPLE
Do not create several unnecessarily different
terms for distinctions that do not serve a
real classificatory purpose.

subordinate classes.

of the subject structure.

Personality or Matter component at different
structural levels.

A subject may refer to one geographical
context and one time context and then contain
a further spatial/temporal qualification.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
79 POSTULATE OF LEVEL
Example

A broad subject may contain progressively
deeper levels of specification.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
80 POSTULATE FOR FACET SEQUENCE
Example

Once the appropriate facet sequence is
determined for a class of subjects, the same
basis is applied consistently.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
81 POSTULATE OF FIRST FACET
Example
EXAMPLE
The facet selected as first is chosen because
of its structural/conceptual priority.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
82 POSTULATE OF CONCRETENESS
Example
EXAMPLE
A facet representing a concrete entity may
precede a more abstract qualification where

└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Final qualifying facets are arranged in the

└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Related facets occurring at one level of a
compound subject are kept together rather
than being scattered arbitrarily.

5.2 PRINCIPLES FOR FACET SEQUENCE 4
86 WALL-PICTURE PRINCIPLE
Example
EXAMPLE
In arranging related facets, the sequence
should present the subject in a natural
coherent view rather than an arbitrary
order.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
87 WHOLE-ORGAN PRINCIPLE
Example
EXAMPLE
When classifying organs of an organism,
their sequence can follow the organization of
the whole organism rather than an arbitrary
order.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
88 COW-CALF PRINCIPLE
Example
EXAMPLE
A dependent component should not be separated
arbitrarily from the component with which it
has the closest natural relationship.
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
89 ACTAND–ACTION–ACTOR–TOOL PRINCIPLE
Example
EXAMPLE
In a subject concerning treatment, the thing
acted upon, the treatment/action, the person
performing it and the instrument used can be
related in this conceptual sequence.
