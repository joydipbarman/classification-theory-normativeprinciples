55

PRINCIPLE OF LATER-IN-TIME

Definition: Subjects or isolates that originated at different times should, in parallel, be arranged progressively according to time, unless an overwhelming consideration rules it out.
Example: Vedic → Post-Vedic → Jainism → Buddhism → Judaism → Christianity → Islam This follows chronological progression.
56

PRINCIPLE OF LATER-IN-EVOLUTION

Definition: Subjects or isolates belonging to different stages of evolution should be arranged parallel to the evolutionary sequence, unless an overwhelming consideration rules it out.
Example: Thallophyta → Bryophyta → Pteridophyta → Gymnosperm → Monocotyledon → Dicotyledon Embryo → Child → Adolescent → Old
57

PRINCIPLE OF SPATIAL CONTIGUITY

Definition: Entities or subjects associated with geographically contiguous regions may be arranged in corresponding spatial order.
Example: Countries or regions may be arranged according to their geographical contiguity when this gives a helpful sequence.
58

PRINCIPLE OF BOTTOM-UPWARDS

Definition: Arrange entities in a spatial sequence from the lower position upward when that direction is the helpful one.
Example: Places in a vertical structure may be arranged from lower level → higher level.
59

PRINCIPLE OF TOP-DOWNWARDS

Definition: Arrange entities from a higher position downward when that direction is more helpful.
Example: Levels of a building may be arranged from upper floor → lower floor.
60

PRINCIPLE OF LEFT TO RIGHT

Definition: Where spatial direction is relevant, arrange items from left toward right.
Example: Positions on a diagram may be sequenced left → centre → right.
61

PRINCIPLE OF RIGHT TO LEFT

Definition: Where appropriate, arrange items spatially from right toward left.
Example: A spatial array may be organized right → centre → left when that direction is more helpful for the universe concerned.
62

PRINCIPLE OF CLOCKWISE DIRECTION

Definition: Arrange entities around a circular or spatial sequence in clockwise order when appropriate.
Example: Positions around a circular arrangement may be sequenced clockwise from a selected point.
63

PRINCIPLE OF COUNTER-CLOCKWISE DIRECTION

Definition: Arrange entities around a circular sequence in counter-clockwise order when that is the helpful direction.
Example: A circular geographical or positional array may be traversed counter-clockwise.
64

PRINCIPLE OF PERIPHERY TO CENTRE

Definition: Arrange spatial entities from the outer region toward the centre.
Example: Concentric geographical zones may be arranged outer zone → inner zone → centre.
65

PRINCIPLE OF CENTRE TO PERIPHERY

Definition: Arrange spatial entities from the centre outward toward the periphery.
Example: Concentric regions may be arranged centre → inner zone → outer zone.
66

PRINCIPLE OF AWAY-FROM-POSITION

Definition: Arrange entities progressively away from a specified reference position.
Example: Places may be arranged according to increasing distance from a central reference point.
67

PRINCIPLE OF INCREASING QUANTITY

Definition: Arrange entities in increasing order of a quantitative characteristic.
Example: Population: 1 million → 5 million → 10 million.
68

PRINCIPLE OF DECREASING QUANTITY

Definition: Arrange entities in decreasing order of a quantitative characteristic.
Example: Population: 10 million → 5 million → 1 million.
69

PRINCIPLE OF INCREASING COMPLEXITY

Definition: Arrange subjects or entities from simpler forms toward increasingly complex forms.
Example: Simple structure → compound structure → increasingly complex structure.
70

PRINCIPLE OF CANONICAL SEQUENCE

Definition: Where a recognized traditional or established canonical order exists, the classes should follow that accepted sequence when useful.
Example: A discipline's established conventional order may be retained instead of creating an arbitrary new sequence.
71

PRINCIPLE OF LITERARY WARRANT

Definition: The sequence or provision of classes should be supported by the existence and distribution of literature in the subject field.
Example: If a subject has a large and established body of literature, it deserves appropriate provision and sequence in the classification scheme rather than being treated as though it had no literature.
72

PRINCIPLE OF ALPHABETICAL SEQUENCE

Definition: When no stronger or more useful sequence is available, entities may be arranged in alphabetical order.
Example: Countries: Bangladesh → Bhutan → India → Nepal → Pakistan The sequence is alphabetical.
